Protocol Title: Measurements of decision-making rationality
Principal Investigator: Alexander Friedman
UTEP: Biology

Introduction

You are being asked to take part voluntarily in the research project described below. You are encouraged to take your time in making your decision. It is important that you read the information that describes the study. Please ask the study researcher or the study staff to explain any words or information that you do not clearly understand.

Why is this study being done?

Approximately 350 adult individuals (18 years of age and older) will participate in this study.
The purpose of this study is to evaluate how individuals process information and make choices when presented with multiple options simultaneously. Traditional decision-making models often assume that people evaluate choices against fixed, rigid preferences. This research aims to collect empirical data on how choice context and option presentation influence decision-making strategies across everyday scenarios.

We will chart subjects’ valuation and decision-making at a strategic level. We will identify the imbalanced decision-making observed in healthy human subjects such as overvaluation of rewards, cost, or risk in decision-making. We will use a combination of neuronal network, algorithmics and analytic models to classify the psychometric functions in our subjects. To identify the patterns of striosomal circuit activation that led to optimal cost-benefit decision-making, we will prove, improve, or adjust our current model.

What is involved in the study?

If you agree to participate, you will complete a single, online behavioral questionnaire hosted on a secure web platform. Participation involves a single session that takes approximately 30 minutes to complete. There are no repeated visits or multi-day commitments. You will first be asked to assign basic preference ratings to a series of everyday, simple items or scenarios. Following this, you will complete a multiple-choice section selecting between different combinations of those same items. You will also answer a few basic background questions (e.g., gender/sex). All tasks are completed asynchronously on your own computer or mobile device in a location of your choosing. No physical equipment, physiological recording, or face-to-face interaction is required.

What are the risks and discomforts of the study?

The risks associated with this research are minimal and no greater than those encountered in daily life or during routine computer use. As with any extended study you may experience minor frustration, fatigue, and discomfort. The questionnaire items consist of simple, non-sensitive everyday scenarios. As with any online activity, there is a minimal risk of a breach of confidentiality. Strict data de-identification and security protocols are in place to minimize this risk.

What will happen if I am injured in this study?

Because this study consists solely of an online survey, the risk of research-related injury is extremely low. However, The University of Texas at El Paso and its affiliates do not offer to pay for or cover the cost of medical treatment for research-related illness or injury. No funds have been set aside for this purpose. You do not give up any of your legal rights by signing/agreeing to this consent form. If you believe you have experienced a study-related issue, report it to Dr. Alexander Friedman at (857)-233-1843, and to the UTEP Institutional Review Board (IRB) at (915)-747-6590 or irb.orsp@utep.edu.

Are there benefits to taking part in this study?

There are no direct benefits for taking part in this study.

What are my costs?

There are no direct costs.

Will I be paid to participate in this study?

You will not receive direct monetary compensation for taking part in this study. If you were recruited through a course offering extra credit, details regarding extra credit options (and non-research alternative assignments) will be provided directly by Dr. Friedman.

What other options are there?

You have the option not to take part in this study. There will be no penalties involved if you choose not to take part in this study
Choosing to withdraw or not participate will not affect your grades, class, or university standing.
Choosing to withdraw or not participate will not affect your employment, departmental, or university standing.

What if I want to withdraw, or am asked to withdraw from this study?

Taking part in this study is voluntary. You have the right to choose not to take part in this study. If you do not take part in the study, there will be no penalty or loss of benefit.

If you choose to take part, you have the right to stop at any time.

Who do I call if I have questions or problems?

If you have any questions, you may call Dr. Friedman at (857) 233-1843 and afriedman@utep.edu.

If you have questions or concerns about your participation as a research subject, please contact the UTEP Institutional Review Board (IRB) at (915) 747-6590 or irb.orsp@utep.edu.

What about confidentiality?

Your part in this study is confidential. The following procedures will be followed to keep their personal information confidential.

The results of this research study may be presented at meetings or in publications; however, your name will not be disclosed in those presentations.

Every effort will be made to keep your information confidential. Your personal information may be disclosed if required by law.

Organizations that may inspect and/or copy your research records for quality assurance and data analysis include, but are not necessarily limited to:

  • Office of Human Research Protections
  • UTEP Institutional Review Board
Because of the need to release information to these parties, absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed.

All research involved will keep detailed electronic experimental data stored in a database. These notes will have sequentially marked pages, where any experiment will be documented in detail. All notes and protocols during experiments will be collected by our software. The database will be managed by MATLAB and Proc SQL functions. All notes will be recorded and updated daily. It will be stored on the UTEP server. Data on the server will be backed up weekly. We will create a detailed protocol description for all functions and data as routinely done for our past publications. We will use read-me files to create a supplemental material document with a description of all the functions and procedures. Data generated from this project will be administered in accordance with both University and NSF policies, including NSF data sharing policy and implementation guidance. Friedman Laboratory follows an open data philosophy. Therefore, in our previous publications and this proposal, all data and protocols without exclusion will be deposited into the Mendeley Database. All codes used to analyze and process the data will be submitted to GitHub database. We will create special scripts that generate published figures and panels from raw data and include statistical analysis. Any inventions developed under this project will be reported and managed as provided by NSF policies. The electronic version of the final, upon acceptance for publication will be deposited to the National Library of Medicine’s Pubmed Central to be made publicly available no later than one year after the official date of publication. Both the PI and the University of Texas at El Paso will have responsibilities, and hence rights, concerning access to, use of, and maintenance of original research data.
